# Gutshot — Narrative Reference

## Setting

Hive cities: vertical arcologies housing hundreds of millions, stratified by wealth. The upper
spires gleam; the underhive rots. Think Warhammer 40k density, cyberpunk aesthetic — neon over
rust, chrome over bone. Corporate enclaves sit behind killwalls. Gangs run the transit tunnels.
Cults bleed through the walls at night.

Tone: grim but not hopeless. The crew swears, laughs, and bleeds. Small victories matter.
Reference points: The Expanse (grounded sci-fi grit, crew loyalty) meets Firefly (outsider
perspective, sharp banter, found-family dynamics).

---

## The Crew

A small crew of 4-6 outlaws — mercenaries, deserters, smugglers, and worse. They're not heroes.
They take the jobs nobody else will, because nobody else will have them.

2. The Gunner — former hive gang enforcer, loud, loyal to a fault
3. The Medic — field-trained, black-market implants, running from something
4. The Hacker — young, clever, in over their head
5. The Veteran — oldest crew member, seen everything, says little
6. The Wild Card — (optional sixth slot) — background TBD per campaign

Characters have personalities but no scripted dialog. The player voices them through choices
and loadouts. Relationships shift based on mission outcomes.

---

## Antagonist

A demon cult with deep corporate backing. The cult provides the fanaticism and the bodies; the
corporate backer provides money, weapons, and official cover. They are dismantling something —
a person, a place, a secret — and the crew gets pulled into stopping them because someone they
know is in the way.

The cult is not cartoonishly evil: they believe what they're doing is necessary. That's worse.

---

## Mood Notes

- Violence has weight. No quipping over fresh corpses.
- The hive city is almost a character: oppressive, alive, indifferent.
- The crew's ship/hideout is the one place that feels safe. Protect that.
- End on something earned, not something easy.
